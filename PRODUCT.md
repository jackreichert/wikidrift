# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Readers, researchers, and Wikipedia contributors use WikiDrift to inspect how an article changed over time and how language editions differ. They need a fast overview, followed by direct access to the underlying wording, versions, citations, and model-assisted comparisons.

## Product Purpose

WikiDrift turns public Wikipedia history into reproducible research leads. Success means helping a reader identify what deserves closer inspection without converting change, disagreement, editor activity, or missing analysis into a judgment of bias or bad faith.

## Brand Personality

Forensic, cautious, and transparent. The product should feel technically credible while remaining readable to people who do not know the underlying metrics.

## Anti-references

WikiDrift must not resemble a bias score, a blacklist, an accusation engine, or a sensationalized controversy dashboard. It must not hide uncertainty behind simplified labels or imply that an analysis ran when only presentation data is missing.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve evidence states from data to headline: not run, candidate, confirmed, agree, compatible difference, contradiction, and insufficient are distinct.
2. Lead with plain language, but retain the metric's actual meaning and uncertainty.
3. Keep every summary traceable to inspectable text, revisions, citations, or structured findings.
4. Treat disagreement and editor activity as context, never proof of intent.
5. Make missing coverage visible instead of turning absence into a negative finding.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA intent. Keep all findings usable by keyboard, preserve visible focus, avoid color-only status communication, provide semantic table structure, and maintain readable contrast and text sizing across desktop and mobile layouts.
