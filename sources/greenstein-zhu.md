# Greenstein & Zhu — political slant on Wikipedia

## Paper 1 — Is Wikipedia Biased?

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | Shane Greenstein, Feng Zhu |
| **Venue** | *American Economic Review* P&P, 2012 |
| **Link** | https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.102.3.343 |

### Summary

Measures **political slant** of ~28k US-politics articles using Gentzkow–Shapiro-style phrase indexes (language associated with Democratic vs Republican congressional speech).

**Findings:**

- Early Wikipedia leaned Democrat on average.
- Average slant **moved toward neutral** over a decade.
- Rebalancing came **mostly from new articles with opposite slant**, not from heavy revision of existing pages.
- **Most articles change only mildly from their initial slant.**

## Paper 2 — Do Experts or Crowd-Based Models Produce More Bias?

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | Shane Greenstein, Feng Zhu |
| **Venue** | *MIS Quarterly*, 2018 (earlier WP 2014) |
| **PDF** | http://fengzhu.info/BritannicaWikipedia.pdf (common mirror) |

### Summary

Compares matched **Wikipedia vs Encyclopædia Britannica** articles on US politics for slant and bias (absolute slant).

**Findings:**

- Wikipedia more slanted Democrat and more biased on average than Britannica in the sample.
- Bias gap shrinks with more revisions.
- Per-word bias is closer (Wikipedia articles are longer).

## Relation to WikiDrift

**Load-bearing caution:** *detecting a rewrite ≠ detecting bias.*

If per-article slant rarely flips via revision, then a large pivot is a **change** candidate. Direction and capture need other instruments (L2/L5). Base-rate result (Climate change out-pivoting Zionism) agrees. Explicitly baked into product framing: not a bias oracle.
