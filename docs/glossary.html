Reading tips

You should not need a special dictionary for this site. If a page still feels dense, these short reminders may help.

“Finding” or “lead”

Something worth opening and checking. Not a conviction, and not proof of bad faith.

“Rewrite” (sometimes called a pivot in research notes)

A stretch of time when a large share of wording that had been stable for a long time was replaced and the replacement stayed. Open the before-and-after view and read it yourself.

“Overview”

The short briefing at the top of an article page. Begin there; use the other tabs only if you want detail.

“Framing”

How the writing presents a subject — more critical, more sympathetic, or more neutral — beyond the bare facts. Two languages can agree on a date and still frame the story differently.

“Facts check”

A short list of basic questions (who / when / where / how many). Editions may agree, differ, contradict, or simply not say enough to tell (insufficient). That is not a score of who is right.

“Citations changed”

Which domains and types of sources (books, news, journals, web pages) grew or shrank across a rewrite. We show the mix only. We do not mark sources as trustworthy or untrustworthy.

“Vocabulary shift”

Which words became more or less common. Helpful for noticing a topic change; easy to over-read. Ignore it if the rewrite text already makes the story clear.

“Concentrated authorship”

A large share of today’s text comes from a small number of accounts. Common on specialist pages. Context only — not evidence of a conspiracy.

“Heavily contested” vs “not heavily contested”

Whether editors spent a lot of effort undoing each other. A quiet rewrite can still matter. A loud fight does not prove anyone is wrong.

“Found via discovery”

This page showed up because large deletions on another related article pointed investigators here. It was then checked on its own history. The trail never condemns a page by itself.

“Control” topics

Quiet subjects (for example basic science) included so we can see what ordinary long-term editing looks like — and avoid treating every big rewrite as a scandal.

If you only do three things

  1. Read Overview.
  2. Open Rewrite and read the actual text change.
  3. Click through to Wikipedia when you want the original version.
