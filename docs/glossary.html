Glossary

Plain-language definitions. These terms are linked throughout the article pages.

Lead (not a verdict)
Every result here points you somewhere to look. It is never proof that an article is biased or that anyone acted in bad faith.
Drift
When an article's narrative shifts over time as it is edited.
Pivot
A specific moment when an article was heavily rewritten, with a lot of long-standing text replaced at once. Pivots appear on the timeline in the Diff tab.
Framing
How a subject is portrayed, leaning critical, neutral, or sympathetic, beyond the plain facts. Different editions can frame the same topic differently.
Stance / neutral point of view
We rate framing on a neutrality scale (critical, neutral, sympathetic), not "positive / negative sentiment", which can mislead.
Cross-lingual comparison
Comparing the same article across languages. When independent editions disagree, that disagreement is informative.
Fact divergence
When editions state incompatible facts (for example, very different numbers). Distinct from framing: editions can agree on facts yet frame them differently.
Citation-source change
What an article's own citations changed from → to across a major rewrite — which source-domains it added or dropped, and the shift in citation types (journal, news, book, web). We deliberately do not label any source reliable or biased: that judgment is itself contested and would make the tool dismissible. We show the composition and its change as-is, and you judge.
Blame
Like a version-control "blame": for each piece of text, which editor introduced it.
Editor concentration
How much of the article's current text a small number of editors wrote — e.g. the top-10 editors' share, alongside the total count of distinct editors. A high share with few distinct editors means fewer independent eyes on the text (low authorship diversity), which is one context signal among several — descriptive, not a verdict.
Born-framed
An article written with a strong point of view from the start, so there is no earlier version to compare against. The disagreement is inborn, not introduced at a pivot.
Control
A neutral topic (for example, Photosynthesis) we expect to show little disagreement, a sanity check that the tool is not crying wolf.
Conflict weight
A measure of how much an article was fought over (edit-wars). High means contested; it does not mean biased.
Receipts
The exact Wikipedia revisions (with dates) each finding was computed from, so anyone can verify.
Stability prior
The idea that text which survived years of readers and editors has earned trust. When a long-stable passage is suddenly removed or reversed and the change sticks, that breaks the prior, which is what makes it worth a look.
Persistence-weighted loss (PWR)
How we measure a rewrite: each word counts for how long it had survived, so deleting 20-year-old text weighs far more than churning last month's. Grounded in published Wikipedia content-survival research, not an arbitrary rule.
Capture by change vs. by origin
Two ways an article can end up skewed. By change: it was rewritten over time (this tool catches that). By origin: it was written with a point of view from the start, or a like-minded majority kept it that way, so there is no earlier version to compare against, which needs an external reference.
Base rate
Big rewrites are common and usually legitimate. Before calling any single change meaningful, you have to know how often change happens anyway. The largest rewrite in our test set was a perfectly benign one.
External reference
Something outside the article's own history to compare against, such as other language editions or independent encyclopedias. The only way to judge bias that was there from the start.
Conjunction
No single signal is proof. A finding is only strong when several stack together, for example: long-stable text, removed, reversed in meaning, and kept despite others trying to revert it.

A compilation of findings from the WikiDrift tool. Every result is a lead for a researcher, never a published verdict: a change-and-disagreement detector, not a bias oracle. Editor-agnostic, from public data only.
