Cross-language lead comparison

How the opening of the article presents the topic in different languages. Disagreement is a reason to read carefully, not a final answer.

The English lead text was unchanged between the two revisions in this window, so there is no English-side temporal movement to report. Comparisons across editions show persistent, pre-existing differences: Arabic frames the topic as 'the Palestinian cause/Arab-Israeli conflict' with a historical-humanitarian scope from 1880, Hebrew frames it as a conflict between Jewish and Arab residents of the Land of Israel, and Japanese frames it as a political problem arising from relations between Palestinian settlers and Zionist Jewish settlers—all differing from the English framing of a two-sided but internally diverse conflict between the State of Israel and Palestinian Arabs. These differences existed both before and after the window in each edition, indicating persistence rather than convergence or divergence during this period.

Cross-language lead comparison

How the opening of the article presents the topic in different languages. Disagreement is a reason to read carefully, not a final answer.

The English, Arabic, Japanese, and Odia lead sections show no substantive textual change between the BEFORE and AFTER revisions in this window; the edits appear to be minor (e.g., a single non-breaking space added in Odia). The persistent cross-edition differences are structural: English frames the topic strictly as a land/self-determination conflict since the late 19th/early 20th century, while Arabic frames it as 'القضية الفلسطينية' rooted in 1897 and central to the broader Arab-Israeli conflict, a difference that predates and persists through this time window with no convergence or divergence detected.
