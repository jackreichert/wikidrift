Where the openings part ways

How the opening of the article presents the topic in different languages. Disagreement is a reason to read carefully, not a final answer.

Over this window, the English lead shifted substantially toward a more contested framing: it downgraded Palestine from a 'de jure sovereign state' to a 'disputed and partially recognized state', added mention of the PLO's terrorist-organization designation, reattributed the 1967 territorial situation to Jordan's prior occupation/annexation and its attack on Israel rather than simple Israeli occupation after the Six-Day War, and reassigned the 2011 UN membership application from the 'State of Palestine' to the 'Palestinian Authority'. The French edition also moved in a similar contested direction (adopting 'partiellement reconnu' and citing Jordanian-era control), representing a parallel change, though its actor for the UN application differs from English's revised claim. The Arabic, Greek, and Hebrew leads remained essentially textually static across the same period, so no comparable shift occurred there, meaning the observed divergences are mostly cases of English moving away from earlier neutral state-status language while non-English editions (except French) did not follow or already lacked comparable content.
