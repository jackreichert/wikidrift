Where the openings part ways

How the opening of the article presents the topic in different languages. Disagreement is a reason to read carefully, not a final answer.

The three leads are substantively aligned in content, structure, and argument. The main divergence is that the SR and KO editions explicitly mention that fascist nationalism included racial nationalism and religious nationalism as subtypes, a specification absent in the EN lead. Additionally, the SR edition frames the non-racial conceptions of nationhood as 'non-racist' rather than EN's more neutral 'non-racialized,' a subtle difference in connotation. These are minor wording/emphasis differences rather than factual contradictions, and the overall narrative, causal attributions, and characterizations of fascism's ideological history remain consistent across editions.
