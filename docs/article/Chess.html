Where the openings part ways

How the opening of the article presents the topic in different languages. Disagreement is a reason to read carefully, not a final answer.

The EN, UK, and FR leads share the basic definition of chess and its historical link to chaturanga, but diverge notably on framing and detail. EN emphasizes computer chess history (Deep Blue, Kasparov) and modern relevance to science/psychology, none of which UK mentions and FR only briefly touches. UK omits the current World Champion and Deep Blue entirely, but uniquely includes detailed practical information on time controls (classical, rapid, blitz, bullet) and game phases (opening, middlegame, endgame) not found in EN or FR. FR and UK both offer different accounts of chess's arrival in Europe (Umayyad conquest in Spain in the 9th century vs. Arab introduction in the 10th century) that diverge from EN's more general Persia-to-Arab-world-to-Europe narrative. FR alone cites early competitive chess history (Harun al-Rashid's court, 1851 London tournament) and the Association of Chess Professionals, absent from EN and UK.
