The drop is the percentage-point decline in how much of that wording survives across the whole candidate window. The linked before-and-after revision pair locates the dominant step within that window; the percentage still measures the whole window. It measures established wording loss, not whether the resulting text is better, worse, more neutral, or less neutral.

Cross-language lead comparison

How the opening of the article presents the topic in different languages. Disagreement is a reason to read carefully, not a final answer.

The EN edition provides extensive detail on forced assimilation, colonization history, language suppression, and specific population statistics, none of which appear in the KA or JA versions. The JA edition instead emphasizes Jomon ancestry theory and cultural continuity with mainland Japanese and Ryukyuans, and cites specific 2019 legislation and a 2020 cultural institution absent from EN. The KA edition is the briefest, characterizing Ainu primarily through subsistence practices (hunting, fishing, agriculture) and omits nearly all modern political, demographic, and historical assimilation context found in EN and JA. Overall, the editions diverge mainly through omission and differing thematic emphasis rather than direct contradiction, though this reflects distinct editorial priorities in origin narrative versus modern political/historical framing.
