Where the openings part ways

How the opening of the article presents the topic in different languages. Disagreement is a reason to read carefully, not a final answer.

The English edition provides a substantially more detailed and historically framed account, emphasizing forced assimilation, colonization, population statistics, and the decline/extinction of Ainu languages. The Japanese edition instead emphasizes ethnogenesis (Jōmon lineage), legal recognition status, and modern cultural institutions (Upopoy, national museum), largely omitting the colonization and language-suppression narrative found in EN. The Georgian edition is the most divergent, offering a brief and somewhat different characterization centered on subsistence lifestyle, first-settler claims, and internal terminological disputes about Kuril Ainu, while omitting nearly all modern historical, political, and demographic content present in EN and JA.
