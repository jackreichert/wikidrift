About WikiDrift

A change-and-disagreement detector for Wikipedia. It surfaces where an article's story shifted, or where language editions tell different stories, so a person can take a closer look.

A lead for a researcher, not a verdict.

What this is

Wikipedia is written and rewritten by volunteers, an extraordinary effort begun in good faith. But it is also human, self-governed, and subject to bad actors: its editorial disputes are settled by consensus among whoever shows up, and its governance is contested. So an article's story can quietly shift: text that stood for years gets rewritten, or the different language editions of the same topic tell noticeably different stories. WikiDrift surfaces those shifts and disagreements so a person can investigate.

What it does, and does not, claim

It is a change-and-disagreement detector, not a bias detector. Finding that an article changed a lot, or that editions disagree, is a lead, never a conclusion that anyone did something wrong. Big, legitimate rewrites happen all the time. We never accuse individual editors.

How it reads an article

  • Its own history: the moments an article was most heavily rewritten (its pivots), shown as a before / after.
  • Other language editions: how the English and, say, Hebrew / Arabic or Polish / German editions frame the subject, and whether they agree on the facts.
  • Who wrote what: blame shows which editor introduced each part.

Why "make disagreement legible"

There is rarely a single neutral truth. Rather than declare one, WikiDrift shows where independent sources disagree, with the receipts, and lets you judge. It uses only public data and is open-source.

For how the detection actually works, the layers involved, and the discipline that keeps every result a lead rather than an accusation, see the Methodology.
