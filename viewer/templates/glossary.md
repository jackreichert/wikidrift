# Reading tips

<p class="summary">You should not need a special dictionary for this site. If a page still feels
dense, these short reminders may help.</p>

## “Finding” or “lead”

Something worth opening and checking. **Not** a conviction, and **not** proof of bad faith.

## “Rewrite” (sometimes called a pivot in research notes)

A stretch of time when a large share of wording that had been stable for a long time was replaced and
the replacement stayed. Open the before-and-after view and read it yourself.

## “Candidate” vs “confirmed”

A **candidate** is a time window selected for closer inspection. It may come from one sharp interval or
from sustained loss across roughly twelve months. **Confirmed** means the underlying revision history
also shows the required collapse of long-lived wording. Neither term means the rewrite was biased.

## “Overview”

The short briefing at the top of an article page. Begin there; use the other tabs only if you want
detail.

## “Framing”

How the writing presents a subject — more critical, more sympathetic, or more neutral — beyond the
bare facts. Two languages can agree on a date and still frame the story differently.

## “Facts check”

A short list of basic questions (who / when / where / how many). Editions may **agree**, **differ**,
**contradict**, or simply not say enough to tell (**insufficient**). That is not a score of who is right.

## “Citations changed”

Which domains and types of sources (books, news, journals, web pages) grew or shrank across a rewrite.
We show the mix only. We do **not** mark sources as trustworthy or untrustworthy.

## “Vocabulary shift”

Which words became more or less common. Helpful for noticing a topic change; easy to over-read.
Ignore it if the rewrite text already makes the story clear.

## “Concentrated authorship”

A large share of today’s text comes from a small number of accounts. Common on specialist pages.
Context only — not evidence of a conspiracy.

## “Exact-event attribution”

For one confirmed event, the public edit history is read as an ordered sequence between stable before
and after states. The receipt separates every observed addition, removal, and restoration from the
removals and replacement wording still standing at the after state. Raw per-revision counts reproduce
the displayed shares. It describes observable revision history, not bias, motive, coordination, or
misconduct.

## “Heavily contested” vs “not heavily contested”

Whether editors spent a lot of effort undoing each other. A quiet rewrite can still matter. A loud
fight does not prove anyone is wrong.

## “Found via discovery”

This page showed up because large deletions on another related article pointed investigators here.
It was then checked on **its own** history. The trail never condemns a page by itself.

## “Control” topics

Quiet subjects (for example basic science) included so we can see what ordinary long-term editing
looks like — and avoid treating every big rewrite as a scandal.

## If you only do three things

1. Read **Overview**.
2. Open **Rewrite** and read the actual text change.
3. Click through to Wikipedia when you want the original version.
